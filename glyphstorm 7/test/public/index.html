      Three colors, three jobs

      ◉ Green Your ship, valid words and available pickups. ⚠ Pink Incoming threats, rival pulses and Earth warnings. ◆ Gold Unopened crystals, asteroid labels, map blips and signals. Double outlines mark useful pickups; dashed rings mark low usefulness; WAIT marks reserved letters. Ship names and team labels still identify pilots.

      Dashed pink arrows point from off-screen threats toward Earth’s shield. The red edge warning means Earth is below 30% integrity or a threat is close to its shield. Reduce motion makes this warning steady.

      Quick signals & compact screens

      Tap Signal to ask teammates for a letter or call for Earth defense. Team signals are visible only to your team. In free-for-all, Defend Earth is shared with all pilots; letter requests are disabled. Gold squares on the minimap mark the signal location. Signals expire after 6 seconds and have a 5-second cooldown. They do not transfer letters.

      On compact screens, tap Details for bounties, meteor timers, power-ups, standings, sound and motion preferences. Earth health, boost, your score and the collection board stay on the main screen. Opening Details or Signals stops your controls, but the live round continues and your ship remains vulnerable.

      Scoring a word sends decorative letter energy toward Earth. Your score banner reports the actual HP repaired and shield growth after their caps. Reduced motion replaces the flight with a stationary shield highlight.

      New pilot? Start with one word.

      First Flight teaches moving to a gold crystal, pulsing it, collecting C/A/T, and spelling CAT to repair a practice Earth. Each step unlocks only the controls you need. You can skip it. During a match, optional guided tips explain collection, rearranging and submission. Suggested words and outlined NEXT markers help you find a letter; you can always spell your own word. PULSE markers identify unopened crystals; COLLECT markers identify released letters. Guidance never changes collision pickups.

      The collection board explains when pickup is blocked by a full tray, review, temporary protection or another pilot’s brief priority. Tap × or press Esc to leave review; the world keeps moving while you review.

      1. Open & steer. Aim light pulses at crystals for one letter, or hit an amber asteroid three times to release four letters (including at least one vowel). Gold dots on the minimap mark asteroids. Touch released letters with your ship, then arrange the tray to spell a word. The pilot landing the final pulse and their teammates get 2-second collection priority. Asteroids gently push ships aside without resetting them; only words earn points.
      2. Spell & score. Carry up to 10 letters. Drag tiles on desktop, or tap two tiles to swap on any device. Select range lets you tap a start and end tile; Use all clears the range. The current full tray or selected contiguous range is the submitted word. Unused tiles stay. Green Valid shows the word's points; invalid submissions are disabled. Review shows the highest-scoring Best word. Auto-Best (also labeled Shorter word in review) arranges and selects the longest unused valid word from your tiles. The translucent preview is advice only; press Submit to score. Every word must have 3–10 letters and scores once per pilot per match. Discard any tile with its ×, or select it and press Backspace/Delete. With no tile selected, Backspace discards the last tile. Long-press reveals × on touchscreens. Discard last and Choose a letter to discard remain available. Discards share a one-second cooldown. Shuffle / R is free with a three-second cooldown.
      3. Find your moment. Word review stops your ship and gives up to 8 seconds of protection. Submitting, canceling, or timing out ends it; the cooldown is 15 seconds. You cannot collect or pulse while protected.
      4. Outspell your crew. Highest score when the clock ends wins, provided Earth survives when Planetary defense is enabled. A word scores once per player per match. Teams add their players' points. Ties share the win.
      Planetary defense · survive together, compete in words

      Earth sits at the center of the shared arena. Follow the cyan arrow or minimap ring to find it. Pink incoming letters approach in waves from 12 seconds into the round, then every 8 seconds. Fly into them to collect, then arrange their tiles, or hit them with one pulse to halt their descent and turn them into regular letters. Pulse-stopped letters use the usual 2-second pickup priority.

      Each incoming letter touching the shield is removed and costs Earth 5 HP unless Violet Guard is active. Every valid word repairs 2 HP (maximum 100) and adds half its letter count to shield thickness (maximum 25). Thickness grows the visible shield; ordinary breaches still cost 5 HP. Ordinary crystal, asteroid and dropped letters stay harmless and keep their existing behavior. Ships and pulses can pass over Earth.

      If Earth reaches zero, the round ends for everyone with no winner; earned scores remain visible. Keep it alive until the timer ends to decide the winner by word score. Host can turn Planetary defense off in the lobby for the original timed rules.

      Arcade power-ups · spell five valid words in a row

      In Planetary defense, every fifth consecutive valid word activates a random power-up for 8 seconds. Teammates share a combo; free-for-all pilots build their own. An invalid or already-used word resets that combo. Review, discarding and ship resets do not break it. Power-ups benefit the shared Earth for everyone, including opposing teams. A new milestone replaces the current effect and starts a fresh 8 seconds.

      Violet Guard: Earth’s shield glows violet. Incoming letters touching it disappear without damaging Earth. This protects Earth, not individual ships.

      EMP Blast: all incoming letters stop and become safe pickups, including new waves arriving during the 8 seconds. They keep their positions and are immediately available to everyone. No points are awarded.

      Slow Motion: incoming letters move at 40% of normal speed. Ships, pulses, crystals, asteroids and the match clock keep their normal speed. The original letter speed returns when the effect ends.

      The visible progress meter shows words toward the next power-up. The arcade panel shows your active effect and countdown. Defense-off rounds keep classic scoring without Earth power-ups. Reduced motion stops the moving grid and particles.

      Letter assistance & asteroid rewards

      A Bright double outline: adds a new available word or matches your next suggestion. B Single outline: neutral. Z Dim dashed outline: no immediate valid word with the current letters. Extra tiles never invalidate words you can select from a subset. A small dotted ring marks the gentle magnet: within 72px, the nearest useful released letter drifts toward an eligible ship at 42px/second. Incoming threats retain their normal paths.

      Letters use weighted tile frequencies with a 35–40% vowel target across crystals and released letters. Nearby supply favors vowels below two tray vowels and consonants above four. Only distant unopened crystals may be adjusted to restore the field balance; your collected letters never change.

      When stuck, Smart discard suggests the tile that preserves the most words, then makes the best room for a recovery vowel. Removing a tile cannot create an anagram by itself. After 20 seconds without a pickup or valid submission, a recovery vowel is offered, at most once per 45 seconds. If your tray is full it waits until you discard or submit; no collected tile is silently deleted. Review, resets and disconnection defer automatic delivery.

      Asteroid labels reveal their reward: mostly letters; sometimes an extra vowel, a ★ double-score tile, or a word-shield recharge. A used ★ doubles the word's normal score once; multiple stars do not stack. The +20 bounty remains +20. Stars left outside the submitted range stay in your tray. Shield recharge makes your personal word shield ready, or banks one recharge if it is already active. Rock hits and letter pickup priority are unchanged.

      Boost, meteor showers & word bounties

      Press Shift or tap BOOST for a short dash in your movement direction; when stationary, it follows your ship’s heading. Boost recharges in 6 seconds and gives no protection. It is unavailable during word review, resets or spawn protection.

      Starting 30 seconds into the round, a 14-second meteor shower arrives every minute. Four faster cyan-marked rocks take 4 hits and release 6 letters. Unopened meteors leave at the end of the shower; released letters stay for their normal lifetime.

      Starting 15 seconds into the round, a word bounty appears every 45 seconds. The first pilot to submit that exact word within its 30-second window earns the normal score plus 20 points. Only one pilot can claim each bounty, including in teams. Your once-per-match word rule still applies. Watch the strip above the arena for timers.

      Disruption, when enabled

      Three rival pulse contacts reset your ship for 3 seconds and drop up to 3 letters. Points stay. Dropped letters are immediately shared. Your ship returns with brief protection. Friendly fire is off.

      Your ship: Choose one of eight designs in the lobby, then ready up. Designs are cosmetic: speed, stability and letter collection reach are equal. The ring around each ship shows its pilot color in free-for-all or its team color in team mode. Your choice carries through reconnects and rematches.

      Controls: WASD/arrows to move. Hold Q to rotate counterclockwise or E to rotate clockwise through a full 360°. Move the mouse around your ship to aim freely; hold the left mouse button to pulse. On phones, the left stick moves and the right stick rotates/aims in any direction and pulses. Movement and aiming are independent. Shift or the BOOST button activates a dash. Enter submits the full tray or selected range; R shuffles; Backspace/Delete discards the selected or last tile; Space opens shielded review; Escape resumes flying. Drag tiles or tap two to swap. Use Select range for endpoints.

      The game uses a bundled English dictionary with an offensive-word filter. Proper names and abbreviations are not intended; dictionary coverage may be imperfect. New players arriving mid-round watch until the next lobby.
